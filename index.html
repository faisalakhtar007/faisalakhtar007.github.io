POWER BI

Customer Retention &
Growth Analytics

A 7-page Power BI report analyzing $19.51M in sales uncovering a data integrity issue in the source keys, then using the corrected data to surface retention, concentration, and customer value risks.

Challenge

$19.51M in sales looked strong on the surface, but the Customers table's join key only matched 46% of transactions any retention analysis built on it would have been wrong from the start.

Approach

Identified AltCustomerKey as the true join key before building anything else, excluded an incomplete final month that was distorting growth trends, then built 7 pages covering sales, segmentation, retention, and growth ending with a documented Insights & Recommendations page.

Result

Found Bikes account for ~95% of total revenue (a concentration risk), customers earning $120K+ spend 2.3x more per person than under-$30K customers, and 90-day return rates stay under 10% despite fast new-customer growth each paired with a specific recommendation.

Superstore Sales
Analytics Dashboard

A 3-page Power BI dashboard covering $2.30M in sales across 3 categories and 4 regions tracking sales, profit margin, top products, and customer segments to surface where profitability actually breaks down.

Challenge

$2.30M in sales was split across three categories and four regions, but it wasn't clear where profit was actually being made — some categories looked strong on revenue alone while quietly underperforming on margin.

Approach

Built a 3-page Power BI report (Overview, Product Analysis, Customer Analysis) with Year/Category/Region slicers and a category-by-region profit margin breakdown to separate sales volume from actual profitability.

Result

Found Furniture in the Central region running a −2% margin — losing money — while Office Supplies and Technology delivered 16–24% margins in the same regions, pinpointing exactly where pricing or cost review was needed.

POWER BI

Bank Transaction
Dashboard

A multi-dimensional Power BI report with 9 cross filterable fields and bookmark powered navigation, letting users toggle between line and column chart views on demand.

Challenge

Transaction data spanned multiple banks, cities, and payment channels with no easy way to isolate patterns by customer segment or merchant, or to compare chart formats without cluttering the page.

Approach

Built a Power BI report with 9 cross filterable dimensions (bank sent/received, city, device type, gender, age group, merchant, payment method, purpose, transaction type), and used Bookmarks with button navigation to let users switch between line and column views for amount and transaction count on the same page.

Result

Filtering to a single merchant revealed a sharp mid-year dip and a fairly even distribution of transaction volume across all 4 cities patterns invisible without the drill-down filters, all viewable through a single clean interface instead of separate report pages.
